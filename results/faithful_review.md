**Question:** ¿What's up my usps homies, mi morro es un fan de coleccionar estampillas postales, como puedo comprar unas por el mail esos?

**Retrieved sections:** 18-2.2, 15-2, 18-1, 15-8

**Answer:**


````text

**If the office is designated high-risk:**
- Post **Label 117-A, *Warning: Armed Robbery***, on the rear employee entrances and loading dock doors (per 13-6.1, item e).
- Also post it at the **customer entrances**.

**If the office is not designated high-risk:**
- Post **Label 120, *Facility Alarm***, at the customer entrances (per 13-6.1, item f).
- Label 117-A still goes on the rear employee entrances and loading dock doors, since the rule doesn't limit that placement to high-risk offices (per 13-6.1, item e).

**Other exterior postings required for every office (per 13-6.1):**
- Post Office name, per current Facilities standards
- Hours of operation decal on the main entrance door
- Debit/credit card decal
- Holiday closing sign

**Related note on the alarm system itself:** Only the Postal Inspection Service can evaluate security needs. Don't buy alarms or security systems without its evaluation. Instead, submit a proposal with a detailed justification (per 7-12).
````

## q10  (paraphrase, en)

**Question:** We have a customer angry for not being able to cash a check, in what cases the checks are not payable?

**Retrieved sections:** 11-12.2, 11-12.3, 11-12.1, 4-1

**Answer:**

````text
Per **11-12.2**, employees must not cash the following types of checks:

a. Checks that appear altered.
b. Checks from a third party.
c. Checks for anyone other than the payee(s) named on the face.
d. Checks where the signature on the ID does not match the signature on the check.
e. Checks for more than the amount imprinted as a limit on the face of the document.
f. Checks payable to a business, organization, society, institution, government agency, corporation (LLC or others), or partnership. These must be deposited or negotiated at the customer's own bank or financial institution.
g. Checks issued by an employee.

For detailed procedures on accepting and cashing checks, 11-12.2 refers to **Handbook F-101**.

Some related points may help with the customer:
- **Check acceptance (per 11-12.1):** Checks are not accepted for money order or Sure Money transactions. However, U.S. Treasury checks, traveler's checks, and AMEX gift checks can be used to buy postal money orders. Customer checks must be payable to "U.S. Postal Service" or "Postmaster." Traveler's checks and AMEX gift checks are accepted only if at least 50 percent of the face value is used for Postal Service products or services.
- **Bad check list (per 11-12.3):** Accounting Services removes customers from the list once their debt is paid. Local management may still keep a customer on the list at its discretion.
````

## q11  (direct, en)

**Question:** How can we improve the customer experience at the store, specifically in the wait time line?

**Retrieved sections:** 8-2, 13-3, 13-5, 8-2

**Answer:**

````text
Based on the provided context, you can reduce wait time in line (WTIL) at the Post Office in these ways:

**1. Use lobby assistants**
- Schedule and use lobby assistants, especially during peak times when lines form (per 13-3.b, 8-2).
- Lobby assistants should help customers prepare their transactions before they reach the counter (per 8-2, 13-5).
- This reduces the actual average wait and the *perceived* wait, because it shows customers the Postal Service provides prompt, efficient, and courteous service (per 13-5).

**2. Use self-service and mobile tools**
- Direct customers to the Self-Service Kiosk (SSK) for simple transactions or when lines are long (per 13-3.c, 13-5, 8-2).
- Offer service on a mobile device (mPOS) where applicable, and use mPOS to speed up device-enabled transactions (per 8-2, 13-5).
- Use available equipment (RSS, mPOS, and SSK) to expedite transactions (per 13-3.e).
- Place and maintain self-service equipment properly (per 13-3.d).

**3. Acknowledge waiting customers**
- Lobby assistants and counter clerks should acknowledge customers in line, or apologize when they reach the counter. This shows customers that their time is valued (per 8-2).

**4. Staff and train properly**
- Provide proper training and staff scheduling (per 13-3.a).
- Use the RDM WOS EASG, the F4-SSR D Tool, and the SST to identify proper staffing levels (per 13-3.f).

**5. Promote alternate access**
- Tell customers about other options, such as CPUs, Village Post Offices (VPOs), Stamps by Mail, Click-N-Ship, and www.usps.com (per 13-3.g).

**Goal:** Keep waiting time under 5 minutes (per 13-3). WTIL strongly affects the overall customer experience and whether customers stay with the Postal Service or go to competitors. Great service means respecting a customer's time (per 8-2).
````

## q27  (paraphrase, spanglish, stale_risk)

**Question:** Oye carnal, how can I cash an italian money order in the office, the name in the order seems like a morra, but el que quiere el varo es un carnal?

**Retrieved sections:** 11-14.1, 11-13, 11-14.2, 11-13

**Answer:**

````text
Qué onda, carnal. Esto es lo que dice el manual sobre cambiar giros postales (money orders) nacionales e internacionales:

**Pasos para cambiarlo (per 11-14.1):**
- **Lista de robados:** Revisa que el giro no esté en la lista de Missing, Lost, or Stolen del Postal Bulletin vigente.
- **Monto exacto:** Cámbialo solo por la cantidad exacta impresa al emitirse, hasta el máximo autorizado.
- **Autenticidad:** Revisa las medidas de seguridad por si hay alteración, falsificación o fraude (Notice 299). Si hace falta, llama al Money Order Verification System (866-459-7822).
- **Instrucciones locales:** Sigue también cualquier instrucción adicional del postmaster o del distrito.

**Medidas de seguridad (per 11-14.2):**
Pon el giro contra la luz y busca:
- La marca de agua de Ben Franklin repetida en el lado izquierdo.
- El hilo de seguridad oscuro con las letras "USPS".

Si falta cualquiera de las dos, el giro es fraudulento. Además:
- La denominación aparece en dos lugares y debe ser idéntica.
- Si el monto está decolorado, indica borrado (fraude).

**Sobre el nombre de la morra:**
El manual no menciona específicamente el caso de que el nombre parezca de mujer y lo cobre un hombre. Lo que sí dice es lo siguiente (per 11-14.1):
- Si el giro está a nombre de varias personas unidas con "or", cualquiera de ellas lo puede cobrar.
- Si no hay conjunción, todas las personas nombradas deben firmar y presentar identificación.

Por eso, quien lo cobre debe ser un beneficiario que aparezca en el giro, firmar y presentar su ID. Si el carnal no aparece como beneficiario, el manual no da ninguna base para pagárselo.

**Identificación por monto (per 11-13):**
Si los giros cobrados suman más de $10,000 al día por cliente, se requiere el PS Form 8105-A y una identificación oficial con foto. Si la transacción te parece sospechosa, llena el PS Form 8105-B. Ojo: no le puedes avisar al cliente que lo estás reportando.

**Sobre que sea "italiano":**
El contexto habla de giros postales (postal money orders) internacionales del USPS. No dice nada específico sobre giros emitidos por el correo de Italia. Para eso, consulta el Handbook F-101 (per 11-14.1).
````

**Judge's reason:** The answer mostly follows the context, but it generalizes that any person cashing must be a named beneficiary who signs and shows ID, which the context does not state for single-payee orders.

**Your verdict:** ☐ judge right   ☐ judge too strict

**Note:** 
Judge right, the manual doesn't mention what to do if the person cashing the money order is different than the one in the money order, the judge is right here.
